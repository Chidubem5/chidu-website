About Me

Hey, I'm Chidubem! My name comes from the Igbo language and translates to "God Guide Me" or "I will follow in God's footsteps". It's something I genuinely live by. I hold dual US and Nigerian citizenship, and that blend of cultures has given me a perspective I wouldn't trade for anything.

I studied Physics at Bates College, picking up minors in Computer Science and Mathematics along the way. Right now I'm getting my Master's in Physics at the University of Central Florida, where I work in Dr. Enrique Del Barco's lab on adiabatic spintronics. In plain terms, I'm figuring out how spin currents move through antiferromagnetic materials. I'm also part of NSBP and the Graduate Society of Physics Students, which keeps me connected to a great community of scientists and engineers.

Outside the lab, I love digging into problems from every angle. I went through the Erdős Institute Quant Finance Boot Camp and earned a Google Analytics certification because honestly, I just find patterns fascinating wherever they show up. Financial markets, social dynamics, data pipelines... if there's something to figure out, I'm probably already thinking about it.

I also competed as a collegiate athlete at Bates, playing football as a Defensive End and Outside Linebacker and running track in sprints, high jump, long jump, and shot put. And I've had some incredible travel experiences too. My trips to India and South Africa were service trips through Noble and Greenough School where I got to volunteer in education and community health. Those trips really stuck with me.

Languages: English (Native)  ·  Igbo (Working proficiency)  ·  Spanish (Elementary)

Adiabatic Spintronics

TR-MOKE Measurements

Collaborated on time-resolved magneto-optic Kerr effect measurements with the University of Tampa Optics lab, operating equipment at cryogenic temperatures (~70 K).

Graduate Teaching Assistant
